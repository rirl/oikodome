= Oikodome (οἰκοδομή)
:description: An overview of the Koine Greek word oikodome, its meaning, and its biblical context.
:toc: left
:icons: font

== Etymology & Meaning

The word *oikodome* (written in Greek as *οἰκοδομή*) is a composite noun derived from two distinct Koine Greek roots:

1. *oikos* (οἶκος) – Meaning "house," "home," or "dwelling."
2. *demeō* (δέμω) – A root verb meaning "to build" or "to construct."

Combined, the word carries a dual meaning depending on the context:
* **The Process:** The act of building, construction, or spiritual "building up" (edification).
* **The Product:** The final structure, building, or edifice itself.

== Biblical Usage

In the New Testament, *oikodome* shifts from a literal architectural term to a profound metaphor for community and spiritual growth.

=== 1. Literal Buildings
In the Gospels, it refers to physical structures, most notably the Jerusalem Temple.

[quote, Mark 13:1 (ESV)]
____
And as he came out of the temple, one of his disciples said to him, "Look, Teacher, what wonderful stones and what wonderful **buildings** (*οἰκοδομαί*)!"
____

=== 2. Spiritual Edification
The Apostle Paul frequently uses the word to describe the spiritual development and strengthening of believers.

[quote, 1 Corinthians 3:9 (ESV)]
____
For we are God's fellow workers. You are God's field, **God's building** (*Θεοῦ οἰκοδομή*).
____

[quote, Ephesians 4:12 (ESV)]
____
...for the equipment of the saints for the work of ministry, for **building up** (*οἰκοδομήν*) the body of Christ.
____

== Why Use This for a Project Name?

Using `oikodome` as a repository or project name signifies that the project is:
* **Structural:** It serves as a foundational architecture or framework.
* **Constructive:** It is designed to build something valuable from the ground up.
* **Collaborative:** It represents a unified space where different components work together to form a complete system.
